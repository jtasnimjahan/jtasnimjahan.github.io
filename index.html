About my research

Maternal health, chronic disease, and how we measure risk

I am a PhD student in Epidemiology in the Department of Epidemiology and Biostatistics at the University at Albany, State University of New York. My research sits at the intersection of maternal and perinatal epidemiology, chronic disease epidemiology, and population health measurement.

I trained in clinical medicine in Bangladesh, where my internship in obstetrics and gynecology put me in the room for antenatal visits, deliveries, and postpartum care. I now bring that clinical perspective to population-based research on birth outcomes, obesity, and cardiometabolic health, with a particular interest in how exposure definitions and risk classifications shape what we learn about health disparities.

Studies in progress

Hidden overweight and adverse birth outcomes among Asian mothers in the United States, 2016–2023

Under review

Standard BMI cutoffs were developed largely in non-Asian populations. This population-based reclassification study asks whether applying Asian-specific cutoffs identifies mothers whose overweight is missed by the standard definition, and whether that hidden group faces higher risk of adverse birth outcomes.

Data
U.S. natality records (NVSS), 2016–2023
Methods
Modified PoissonEffect modificationMultiple imputationE-valuesDAG
My role
First author; study design, analysis, and writing
Venue
Paediatric and Perinatal Epidemiology (submitted)

Systems of care for children and youth with special health care needs in New York State, 2016–2024

In preparation

A repeated cross-sectional analysis of how many New York children with special health care needs receive care within a well-functioning system, how that compares with national estimates, and which subgroups are furthest behind. The work is designed to inform Title V planning for 2025–2030.

Data
National Survey of Children's Health; HRSA Title V Information System
Methods
Trend analysisState vs. national benchmarkingSubgroup disparities
My role
Sole author

Who counts as a non-drinker? Reference groups and the alcohol and cardiovascular-kidney-metabolic gradient

In preparation

Studies of alcohol and health often compare drinkers with a "non-drinker" group that mixes lifelong abstainers and former drinkers. This collaborative study examines how the choice of reference group changes estimated associations with cardiovascular, kidney, and metabolic health.

Data
NHANES, 2011–March 2020
Methods
Multi-category exposureReproducibility checks
My role
Co-author; sole analyst for the former-drinker arm (Arm 3)
